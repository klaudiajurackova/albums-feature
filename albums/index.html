Context
A game with events at its core.

Diggy's Adventure is a mobile F2P puzzle game by Pixelfederation. Events are a central part of how the game keeps players engaged. They run on a regular cycle and give players fresh goals outside the main story.

The problem
A familiar event that stopped feeling exciting.

The Tower is a 3-day competitive event where players solve puzzles to climb a leaderboard. Unlike most events that run once, it repeats throughout the year. Its procedurally generated content means it can be produced at scale without significant development cost. After running repeatedly, engagement had dropped. Familiar enough that even the leaderboard competition wasn't pulling players in like it used to.

My role
Design the mechanic's home and everything inside it.

The game designer came up with the Albums mechanic: players find photos hidden across Tower levels and fill themed albums to earn rewards, with three difficulty tiers and a joker card to fill missing slots. My job was to design where it lived and how it worked end to end.

"Players already had a mental model for the Museum, things they'd collected or earned. Albums felt like it belonged there rather than something new to learn."
Research
Look at what exists before designing what's next.

Before opening Figma I looked at how other F2P mobile games handle collection mechanics, mostly to understand layout patterns and what players are already familiar with.

Diggy's Adventure is a complex game with a lot of systems, so I started by mapping where players already went to see things they'd earned. The Camp, the game's home screen, had no space for a new destination, which meant I needed to find an existing one. The Museum already housed achievements, trophies and keepsakes: things players had collected or unlocked. That was the right place. Introducing a new destination would have required players to learn a new behaviour. Placing Albums in the Museum meant zero onboarding cost.

Albums sits as a tab inside the Museum alongside Achievements, Trophies and Keepsakes.
Format
Ground the mechanic in something players already know.

The photo album format was a deliberate choice. Albums was built around the idea of memories, so I wanted the design to connect to something players already understand in real life: pages, bookmarks, photos spread across a spread.

In F2P games players encounter many systems in quick succession. Grounding a new mechanic in something familiar from real life reduces the cognitive effort needed to understand it. Players spend less time figuring out what Albums is and more time engaging with it.

Three difficulty tiers mapped naturally to three pages, with the tier name on the bookmark tab making the hierarchy readable at a glance. Mapping difficulty to physical pages creates a natural sense of progression. Easy fills quickly and gives early reward; hard stays empty longest and creates tension. The physical metaphor carries the emotional arc of the mechanic.

Three pages, three difficulty tiers. Easy fills quickly; hard creates tension.
The joker card
Make the monetisation moment feel like a choice.

The game designer came up with a mechanic where players could fill empty slots they couldn't complete naturally. My job was to make it feel like something players would understand instinctively. I named it the joker card: everyone knows a joker replaces what you're missing.

Consumables that are hard to recover from need a moment of intentionality in the flow. I designed a confirmation step. The joker card creates a pause that respects the player's agency. They chose to use it, they weren't tricked into it.

The confirmation step: a deliberate pause before spending a consumable.
Full feature
Every state, every edge case.

From there I designed the full feature: the Albums tab IA, every state across the lifecycle (event running, nearing end, completed, ended), the reward collection flows, the joker card confirmation, tooltip behaviour, and the onboarding carousel.

Including edge cases like what happens when a player has rewards to collect in both Albums and Achievements at the same time. Achievements has a higher value in the game so that tab opens and the other shows a red notification dot.

Annotated flows covering all states, reward collection, tooltips and onboarding.
